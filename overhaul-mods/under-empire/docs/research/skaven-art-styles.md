# 🐀 Skaven art styles: research for the Under-Empire backdrop

Research date: 2026-11-16. Purpose: inform the art direction of an original, animated SVG warren backdrop (vector shapes, gradients, CSS and SMIL only; dim, dark-mode, behind text). Nothing here is to be copied; styles are described in our own words, and image pages are linked rather than embedded.

## 🔎 Method and evidence levels

- **Observed**: I looked at the image itself in a browser this session and describe what I saw. Most official Skaven images on Warhammer Community carry no artist credit, so observed images are often **uncredited**.
- **Source says**: a claim stated on the linked page.
- **Unverified**: a lead I could not confirm against a primary source.
- Lexicanum (`whfb.lexicanum.com`) returned HTTP 402 to automated fetches, and the Sega Retro artwork category was blocked, so neither was used as a source.

Key primary source: Games Workshop's gallery [Warhammer art through the years: Skaven](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/kbyqkk5e/warhammer-art-through-the-years-skaven/) (17 Apr 2026), 18 images with **no captions or credits**. I viewed all 18. Individual images are referenced below as "WHC gallery #n", following the article's image file order (`aos_whart29-apr17-pic1` to `pic18`).

## 🖋️ 1. Skaven art styles and eras

### A. Late-80s/90s monochrome pencil and ink (Adrian Smith, John Blanche)

- **Credited example**: Adrian Smith, *Skaven Clan Warlord*, 1992, pencil; published in the Warhammer 4th edition Bestiary, *Skaven Armies* 1st edition and White Dwarf 137/153 (source says, collector listing: [2dgalleries](https://www.2dgalleries.com/art/warhammer-skaven-clan-warlord-176350?lang=en)).
- **Observed (that page)**: graphite monochrome; dense, directional hatching; a spiky, crowded silhouette of banners, polearms and a spoked wheel; strong diagonals; the lower third dissolves into massed fur and debris. No colour at all; mood comes from value and line density.
- Wikipedia credits Smith (with Ian Miller) with setting a "dark and serious atmospheric setting" for early Warhammer ([Adrian Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Smith_(illustrator))).
- **John Blanche**: technical pens with watercolour washes until the early 1980s, then inks and acrylic glazes, white acrylic highlights and airbrushed atmosphere; influences include Rackham, Bosch and Dürer; GW art director from 1986; retired 31 May 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Blanche)). Wikipedia also states he died on 1 June 2026 (not checked against another source).
- Blanche's high-concept sketches were the starting point for the 2024 Skaven range, including a skinny giant-rat mount (source says: [Warhammer Studio interview](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/ESNnQAQG/warhammer-studio-interview-designing-the-new-skaven-while-respecting-the-classics/)). I did not see the sketches themselves.
- **Shape language**: jagged, asymmetric, over-filled; texture from line, not colour. **Palette**: graphite/sepia. **Lighting**: implied by hatch density. **Mood**: claustrophobic, grotesque.

### B. 90s-2000s painted "Herohammer" covers (Geoff Taylor, Alex Boyd)

- **Geoff Taylor**, *Warhammer Bestiary Skaven*, 1996, acrylic on artboard; a Skaven band with a leader holding a plague censer whose orb emits green light (source says: [artist's own site](https://geofftaylor-artist.com/galleries/games-workshop/art/warhammer-bestiary-skaven)). Taylor also painted the Black Library *Skavenslayer* cover (2003, acrylic), set in tunnels beneath Nuln ([artist's site](https://geofftaylor-artist.com/galleries/games-workshop/art/skavenslayer)).
- GW describes the Herohammer era as vibrant and dynamic, and the 5th-8th edition army books as bright and colourful ([40 years of Warhammer art](https://warhammer-community.com/2023/10/12/40-years-of-warhammer-an-abridged-history-of-awesome-warhammer-artwork)).
- **Alex Boyd** is credited by GW with many Warhammer Armies covers ([same article](https://warhammer-community.com/2023/10/12/40-years-of-warhammer-an-abridged-history-of-awesome-warhammer-artwork)). That he painted the 2009 *Warhammer Armies: Skaven* cover is **unverified**: the only attribution found is a forum quote of a GW announcement ([forum archive](https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/22266-anyone-recognize-the-coverart)).
- **Karl Kopinski** is described by GW as "gritty, detail-packed" in the 2000s ([same article](https://warhammer-community.com/2023/10/12/40-years-of-warhammer-an-abridged-history-of-awesome-warhammer-artwork)); no source tied him or **Paul Dainton** to specific Skaven pieces (**unverified**).
- **Observed (WHC gallery #8, uncredited, likely this era)**: saturated, high-key fantasy battle painting: orange sky, green mist, red-crested Slayers against a grey rat tide. This is the most "storybook" register and the furthest from what we want.

### C. Age of Sigmar / Skaventide painted illustration (2019-2026)

- 2024 design brief, per the studio: make Skaven "wretched", damp and grimy, "classic low-fantasy", "extremely sinister"; "evolution rather than revolution" on existing iconography ([studio interview](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/ESNnQAQG/warhammer-studio-interview-designing-the-new-skaven-while-respecting-the-classics/)).
- 2024 palette, per 'Eavy Metal: grubby and ramshackle; naturalistic rodent fur thinning to pink skin; weathered/oxidised metals; warpstone ore subtly coloured, refined warpstone "a violent, acid green"; Clan Skryre in pale warm teal with Warlocks in purples and burgundies; Skryre icon is the lightning bolt ([Mustering the Great Clans](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/rW9n7R76/mustering-the-great-clans-find-out-how-eavy-metal-painted-the-hordes-of-skaven/)). (These are miniature painting notes, not illustration notes, but they set the faction's official palette.)
- **Observed (WHC gallery, all uncredited)**: digital painting, loose but detailed; deep near-black shadows with **one dominant sickly green light** (#1, #6, #9, #13, #17, #18); figures emerge from fog rather than being outlined; edges lost into murk; warm accents (red cloth, rust, fire) kept small. #4 is a lighter, vignetted watercolour-like piece with red clan banners and shields over a bluish fog. #13 (a lone assassin) is almost entirely dark green-black with a single rim-lit blade, and is the closest single image to a "dim backdrop" mood.
- Most relevant to architecture: **#2** (Skryre engineers on a ledge overlooking a vertical city, see section 2) and **#14/#18** (green warp-storm landscapes with toppled spires).

### D. Total War: Warhammer (Creative Assembly) environment art

- Simon Pennington (Principal Environment Artist, Creative Assembly) posted in-game Skaven battle maps from the trilogy; credits "Set Dressing and Ruin Assets: David Oxford" and a "Scummy water shader" by himself ([ArtStation](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/EzQ0x2)). The search snippet says he was responsible for the faction's overall look across the series (I did not see that sentence rendered on the page: **partly unverified**).
- **Observed (those maps)**: night scenes in a cold teal-green; massive ruined masonry (older stone cities the Skaven squat in) with crude timber palisades, gates and scaffolds bolted on; large spoked wheels and gears built into walls; a few small green point lights; one warm fire as a counter-accent; mist bands separating depth planes; a tiny moon. Restrained, desaturated, and silhouette-driven: a strong match for our target.
- Other leads not reviewed: Bayard Wu's TWW2 loading-screen illustration "Skaven" ([ArtStationHQ post](https://x.com/ArtStationHQ/status/925000069765615616)); Rinehart Appiah's TWW2 concept "The Great Horned Rat" ([ArtStation](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/x96lR)). **Unverified**.

### E. Vermintide (Fatshark)

- Concept artist Patrik Rosander designed the Skittergate portal and "a Skaven stronghold"; he aimed for lighting variety and more colour than Vermintide 1's muted world ([Fatshark interview](https://www.fatshark.se/news/2018/10/2/hey-there)).
- His process: level-design blockout, 3D Coat sculpt, KeyShot render, Photoshop paint-over, then haze for depth and final light tweaks ([Vermintide dev blog](https://vermintide.com/news/2019/1/18/dev-blog-creating-concept-art-for-vermintide-2)). Level designers deliberately blur foreground and backdrop and use forced perspective ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/warhammer-vermintide-2/vermintide-2-maps)).
- I found **no official Skaven-environment concept art** for Vermintide to review; descriptions of its Skaven tunnels/stronghold look are **unverified**. (A widely shared "Under Empire passage" on ArtStation is explicitly a fan piece inspired by Vermintide 2 and Total War: [Logan Twardowski](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/1454Kq).)

### F. Warhammer: The Old World

- I found no Skaven-specific art or Skaven faction release for The Old World on Warhammer Community; the search turned up only AoS Skaven material. **Unverified / not found.**

## 🏚️ 2. Warren and Under-Empire architecture motifs

Text sources:

- Blight City (AoS): "teetering spires" that collapse soon after building, "stygian dens", gnawholes to other realms, Skryre workshops, built on a colossal warpstone deposit, landmarks that shift weekly ([zero-star review of Blight City](https://warhammer-community.com/2022/06/20/a-zero-star-review-of-blight-city-the-unbelievably-horrific-home-of-the-skaven)).
- Skavenblight (Old World) was the Skaven "main city"; Blight City is a "newly clawed-out sub-realm" ([Complete History of the Skaven](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/3BvT9FYd/a-complete-history-of-the-great-mighty-skaven/)). The same article says bells herald the Skaven's approach and calls the Screaming Bell the faction's most famous centrepiece.
- Skavenblight is hidden in foul marshes on Tilea's border; the Under-Empire tunnels stretch from the Southlands to Kislev and the Far East (a 6th edition rules reference: [6th.whfb.app](https://6th.whfb.app/army/skaven); the site does not name its source book, so treat as secondary).
- Total War: Warhammer III flavour text: the Shattered Tower reaches "from the darkest depths" with "the Bell of the Horned Rat" at its tip ([twdb.io game database](https://twdb.io/warhammer3/database/buildings/wh2_main_special_skavenblight_shattered_tower/)).
- The bell-origin story (a stranger hangs a horned bell on an unfinished tower in Kavzar) is from Lexicanum via search snippet only: **unverified**.
- Hell Pit: no primary source reached. **Unverified.**

Observed in images:

- **WHC gallery #2 (Blight City scene)**: a vertical canyon city; stacked stone towers with tiny lit windows; iron gantries and girder catwalks crossing the sky; arched stone bridges; ropes and cables strung between towers; a riveted, rusted spherical tank in the foreground; a glowing green river at the bottom; green lightning arcing from a mast; thick haze flattening the far planes.
- **Total War maps**: ancient stone ruins re-used; crooked timber palisades, plank gates and lean-to scaffolds; spoked wheels and gears set into walls; skull and spike trophies on poles; green lanterns.
- **WHC gallery #10 (Grey Seer)**: a huge bell in a spoked iron frame with spikes and skulls on top; brass ritual staff with a triangular head.
- **Horned Rat / Skaven glyph (description only)**: on shields, banners and staffs in #4, #5 and #10, a triangle, usually point-down, crossed by three parallel slanted strokes, painted roughly in white or red on red or ochre fields. Clan Skryre's icon is a lightning bolt ([‘Eavy Metal](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/rW9n7R76/mustering-the-great-clans-find-out-how-eavy-metal-painted-the-hordes-of-skaven/)). For our art we should draw an original mark in this spirit, not trace the official one.

Summary of materials: scavenged and re-used stone, crooked planks and beams, rusted iron plates and rivets, girders, chains, ropes and cables, spoked wheels, bells, warpstone glowing through cracks and in vessels, stagnant green water.

## ⚡ 3. Clan Skryre machinery visual language

- Ratling Warpblaster: rotary cannons on a "rickety wooden frame", powered by an overcharged warpstone accelerator battery that must vent gas; it may rattle itself apart ([Ratling Warpblaster article](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/mb0qbJeY/introduce-the-mortal-realms-to-the-true-meaning-of-firepower-with-the-new-ratling-warpblaster/)). The same piece mentions "emerald warpflame".
- Warp-Grinder: powered by a large warpstone crystal projecting a tunnelling beam; the Horned Rat is pictured as a "machine-deity" of cogs, wires and metal ([Clans Skryre article](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/AtzizVc0/the-clans-skryre-assert-their-dominance-with-deadly-machines-and-maniacal-inventors/)).
- Warp Lightning Cannon / Doomwheel: rules describe a warp-lightning blast and an overchargeable warp-lightning generator ([Wahapedia, Warp Lightning Cannon](https://wahapedia.ru/aos4/factions/skaven/Warp-Lightning-Cannon), [Doomwheel](https://wahapedia.ru/aos4/factions/skaven/Doomwheel); third-party rules transcription). I found no official text describing their physical build; brass specifically is **not** mentioned in the 2024 painting notes.
- **Observed**: in WHC gallery #2 engineers carry backpacks with **glass canisters glowing green** inside riveted frames, connected by hoses; a big riveted rusted sphere; masts with lightning. In #6, #9, #11, #14 and #18, warp lightning is **acid yellow-green to near-white at the core**, thin, jagged and many-branched, often with a soft green bloom around it, and it lights the surrounding smoke rather than being a flat colour shape.

How warp lightning reads (observed): a white-hot or pale lime core line; a wider, transparent green halo; fractal branching; short life; it is the brightest value in the image and the only saturated one.

## 🎨 4. Recommendations for a dim vector backdrop

### What makes the current prototype read as cartoony (our analysis)

- Every shape is a clean, closed, flat-filled silhouette with even edges; official art loses edges into fog.
- Too many light sources of equal strength (orange flames plus green glow); official images usually have one dominant light.
- Saturated orange and green at full opacity; official images keep saturation for the warp core only.
- Rounded, symmetric arches and gears look manufactured and friendly; Skaven construction is crooked and patched.
- Rats as full-body silhouettes invite comparison with character art; most official environment scenes show the place, not the inhabitants.

### Traits that translate well to SVG

1. **Atmospheric depth**: 3 to 5 parallax planes, each lighter and greener toward the back, separated by translucent fog bands (`linearGradient` with low-opacity stops). Matches the Total War maps and WHC #2.
2. **One key light**: a single warp source (a cracked vessel, a canal, or a distant tower) as a `radialGradient`; everything else is lit only on edges facing it (rim strokes in a dim lime at 20 to 40 percent opacity).
3. **Lost edges**: fade silhouettes into fog with gradient fills or `mask` gradients, rather than outlining them.
4. **Crooked geometry**: hand-placed polygon points, no perfect arcs, beams slightly off-angle, planks of uneven width, patched plates. Avoid `circle` gears; use toothed polygons with a missing tooth or two.
5. **Texture from line, not noise**: sparse hatching via `pattern` of thin diagonal strokes on shadow planes (nod to style A), plank seams and rivet dots on near planes only. No `feTurbulence` if we want to stay clean and cheap (filters also cost render time behind text).
6. **Desaturated base palette** with one acid accent; warm colour only as a tiny ember counterpoint, if at all.
7. **Warp lightning as a rare event**: a thin pale core path plus a wider blurred-looking halo (a second, wider, low-opacity stroke), flashed via SMIL `opacity` keyframes every 8 to 20 seconds; slow pulse on vessels; drifting fog via `transform` on gradients. Keep motion slow so it never competes with text.

### Candidate direction 1: "Blight City vertical" (painted-illustration register, style C)

A deep canyon of stacked, leaning stone towers and patched iron, crossed by girder catwalks and sagging cables, falling away to a faint green canal at the bottom. Distant towers are pale green-grey shapes in fog; near planes are near-black with rim light. One mast in the mid-distance occasionally discharges warp lightning; glass vessels on a gantry pulse slowly. No visible rats, or only tiny far-off figures on a bridge.

Palette (our derivation):

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Base / near shadow | `#0c100e` |
| Near plane | `#151b18` |
| Mid plane | `#222b25` |
| Far plane / fog | `#3a4a3f` |
| Rim light (use at 30% opacity) | `#8fbf5a` |
| Warp glow | `#6fd13a` |
| Warp core (lightning only) | `#e4ffb0` |
| Rust accent (sparing) | `#5a3622` |

### Candidate direction 2: "Engraver's warren" (pencil and ink register, style A)

A near-monochrome tunnel junction drawn as if in graphite or ink: plank scaffolds and stone arches rendered with hatched shadow planes (SVG `pattern` strokes), crooked outlines of varying stroke width, banners with an original torn glyph. Colour is almost absent; a single green warp source tints the hatching nearest to it. This is the most stylised option but the least cartoony, because the line texture reads as illustration rather than clip-art. It also sits well behind text, since values stay in a narrow band.

Palette (our derivation):

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Paper-dark base | `#100e0b` |
| Shadow plane | `#1d1a15` |
| Hatch / line | `#3b352b` |
| Highlight line (sparing) | `#6e6452` |
| Warp tint | `#5f9a35` |
| Warp core | `#c9f08a` |

### Candidate direction 3: "Night ruin camp" (environment-art register, style D)

A wide, low horizon: old human or dwarf masonry ruins in the distance, with Skaven timber palisades, a crooked gate, a spoked wheel built into a wall, a bell frame on a leaning tower, and skull-topped poles in the middle ground. Cold teal-green night, mist bands between planes, a few small green lanterns that flicker, and one tiny warm brazier as a counterpoint. Calm overall, which suits a backdrop; the warp storm can appear as a faint glow on the horizon.

Palette (our derivation):

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Sky / base | `#0a1312` |
| Far ruins | `#1a2a26` |
| Mid ground | `#13201c` |
| Near silhouettes | `#070c0b` |
| Mist (use at 15-25% opacity) | `#4c6b5e` |
| Lantern green | `#7ee05a` |
| Brazier (tiny) | `#b8642a` |
| Moon / sky glow | `#b9c9a4` |

### Common guardrails (our recommendation)

- Keep the brightest non-lightning pixel at or below about 25% lightness so text stays readable; reserve saturated green for the single key light and lightning.
- Prefer environment over characters; if rats appear, make them small, distant and partly fog-lost, with gaunt, hunched, angular silhouettes rather than round heads and big ears.
- Break symmetry in every arch, gear and tower; add patches, missing planks and sagging lines.
- Draw an original glyph rather than copying the Horned Rat icon.
